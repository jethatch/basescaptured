How it works

Every plate appearance starts with bases up for grabs: four for the batter (all the way home) and, for each runner, the bases left before he scores. The stat is how many of them the play actually took.

Runner on first. A single sends him to third.

The batter takes 1 of his 4 bases. The runner takes 2 of his 3. That plate appearance captured 3 of 7.

A solo home run is 4 of 4. A grand slam is 10 of 10. A strikeout with the bases loaded is 0 of 10.

The measures

The fine print

  • Only the play that ends the plate appearance counts. Steals, wild pitches and pickoffs before it don't.
  • Runners count from where they stand when that last pitch is thrown.
  • A runner thrown out still keeps the bases he reached safely first.
  • Bases taken on errors, dropped third strikes and catcher's interference count, and the player panel says how many.
  • Every plate appearance counts, walks and sacrifices included. Regular season only.
  • That has quirks: a sacrifice bunt that moves a runner up, or an intentional walk with runners on, often scores better than an average plate appearance, while an intentional walk with the bases empty pulls a great hitter down a little.
  • Qualified hitters have 3.1 plate appearances per team game, the same line as the batting title.
